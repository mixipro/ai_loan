# California Rent Control Laws

## Statewide cap — AB 1482

California's Tenant Protection Act (2019) imposes statewide rent control:
- **Annual rent increase cap: 5% + CPI, max 10%**
- Applies to most properties 15+ years old
- Does NOT apply to single-family homes (with restrictions)
- Sunset 2030 (subject to renewal)

## Local rent control (stricter)

These cities have STRICTER local rent control:
- **San Francisco**: 60% of CPI (typically 2-3%)
- **Los Angeles**: 3-8% based on annual basis
- **Berkeley**: CPI-based (typically 3-4%)
- **Oakland**: CPI-based
- **Santa Monica**: CPI-based
- **San Jose**: 5%
- **West Hollywood**: CPI-based

## Just Cause Eviction

AB 1482 also requires "just cause" for eviction after tenancy 12+ months:
- Non-payment of rent
- Breach of lease
- Owner move-in (with restrictions)
- Substantial renovation
- Withdrawal from rental market

NO "no cause" terminations allowed in most cases.

## Exemptions

Properties exempt from AB 1482:
- Single-family homes (if owner is not corporate entity)
- Condos (if owner not corporate)
- Properties built in last 15 years (rolling)
- Duplexes where owner lives in one unit
- Owner-occupied properties

## Implications for investors

1. **Single-family rentals** more flexible than multi-family
2. **Tenant longevity**: Long-term tenants pay below-market rent
3. **Vacancy decontrol**: Rent resets when tenant leaves
4. **Cash flow planning**: Cannot raise rent aggressively
5. **Eviction difficulty**: Even legitimate evictions take months

## Strategy considerations

- Older buildings (subject to local rent control) have lower yield potential
- Newer buildings (15-year exemption) more attractive
- Single-family rentals avoid most rent control
- Plan for rent caps in projections
- Vacancy is opportunity to reset rent to market
- Some investors avoid rent-controlled cities entirely
