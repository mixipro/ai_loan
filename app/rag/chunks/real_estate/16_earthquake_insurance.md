# California Earthquake Insurance

## The big problem

Standard homeowner insurance DOES NOT cover earthquake damage in California.
You need separate earthquake insurance — usually through California Earthquake Authority (CEA).

## Cost

- $800 - $3,000/year for typical home
- Premium depends on:
  - Location (proximity to fault lines)
  - Home age and construction type
  - Foundation type
  - Whether retrofitted

## Deductibles are HIGH

CEA policies have 10-25% deductibles based on COVERAGE LIMIT, not damage.
Example:
- Home insured for $500,000
- 15% deductible policy
- Damage of $80,000
- You pay first $75,000 (15% of $500k coverage)
- Insurance pays only $5,000

This makes coverage often disappointing for moderate damage.

## When to get earthquake insurance

YES, consider it if:
- High earthquake risk area (Bay Area, LA)
- Home is your primary asset
- Older construction (pre-1980)
- Cannot afford to rebuild from savings
- Have valuable contents

PROBABLY NOT if:
- Newer construction (post-1990 with modern codes)
- Have substantial savings/wealth to self-insure
- Modest home value where deductible > likely damage

## Retrofit benefit

California's Brace + Bolt program:
- $3,000 grant for seismic retrofit of older homes
- Reduces insurance premiums significantly
- Strengthens cripple walls and foundation bolts
- ROI: typically 3-5 years in premium savings

## Risk by California region

| Region | Risk | Recommendation |
|--------|------|----------------|
| Bay Area | VERY HIGH | Consider strongly |
| LA Basin | VERY HIGH | Consider strongly |
| San Diego | MODERATE | Optional, evaluate |
| Sacramento | MODERATE | Optional, evaluate |
| Central Valley | LOW | Often skip |
| Central Coast | MODERATE | Evaluate by city |

## Strategy implications

- Factor insurance cost into total ownership cost
- Investment property: typically less critical (depreciation deduction available)
- Primary residence: more critical (your shelter)
- High-value homes: definitely worth considering
