# California REITs and Real Estate ETFs

## Why California REITs

For investors who want California real estate exposure WITHOUT direct ownership:
- No property management hassle
- Liquidity (sell in seconds vs months)
- Diversification (many properties)
- Lower capital requirement
- No earthquake/wildfire risk concentration

## California-relevant REIT options

### Broad US REIT ETFs (much California exposure)
- **VNQ** (Vanguard Real Estate): Largest US REIT ETF
- **SCHH** (Schwab US REIT): Lower fee alternative
- **IYR** (iShares US Real Estate): Diversified

### California-heavy REITs
- **PSA** (Public Storage): Glendale HQ, major CA presence
- **AMT** (American Tower): Headquartered in Boston but big CA footprint
- **EQR** (Equity Residential): Apartments, significant CA exposure
- **ESS** (Essex Property Trust): CA & WA apartments specialist
- **AVB** (AvalonBay): CA apartments specialist

### Specialty California REITs
- **PLD** (Prologis): Industrial real estate, big in Inland Empire
- **MAA** (Mid-America Apartments): Some CA exposure
- **EQIX** (Equinix): Data centers, big in Silicon Valley

## Tax considerations

REITs have unique tax treatment:
- **NO QSBS qualification** (unlike growth stocks)
- **Dividends taxed as ordinary income** (not capital gains rate)
- 20% QBI deduction may apply (Section 199A)
- California taxes dividends as ordinary income (up to 13.3%)
- **NOT tax-efficient for high-bracket Californians**

## Best in tax-advantaged accounts

REITs are MOST efficient in:
- Roth IRA (no tax on dividends or gains)
- 401(k) traditional (deferred tax)
- HSA (potential triple tax-free)
- NOT taxable brokerage account (high tax drag)

## Yield comparison

Typical yields (2024):
- VNQ: ~4% dividend yield
- ESS (CA apartments): ~3.5%
- PLD (industrial): ~3%
- High-yield/mortgage REITs (REM, MORT): 8-12% (high risk)

## Strategy implications

- Use REITs for liquid CA real estate exposure
- Allocate 5-15% of portfolio to REITs
- Always in tax-advantaged accounts when possible
- California-specific REITs not necessary (broad ETFs work)
- Better diversification than direct ownership of one property
