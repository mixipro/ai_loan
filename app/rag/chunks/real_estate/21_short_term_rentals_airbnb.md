# Short-Term Rentals (Airbnb) in California

## The opportunity

Short-term rentals (STRs) in California offer:
- 2-3x revenue vs long-term rental
- Tax advantages (Schedule E vs Schedule C)
- Flexibility (use property yourself)
- Tourist demand year-round

## City-by-city regulations

### Heavily restricted
- **San Francisco**: Owner-occupied only, 90-day limit
- **Santa Monica**: Owner-occupied requirement
- **Los Angeles**: Primary residence requirement, 120-day cap
- **Berkeley**: Permits required, limits enforced

### More permissive
- **San Diego**: Limited permits available, lottery system
- **Palm Springs**: Tourism-friendly, permitted (paid)
- **Lake Tahoe**: Permits required (vacation market)
- **Wine country (Napa, Sonoma)**: Strict but allowed

### Often banned
- **HOA-controlled buildings** often prohibit STRs
- **Most condo associations** ban STRs
- **Some cities ban entirely** (smaller cities)

## Permit costs

- Initial permit: $200-2,000 typical
- Annual renewal: $100-500
- Transient Occupancy Tax (TOT): 10-15% of revenue
- Business license: $50-200/year

## Income potential

Average California STR income:
- Lake Tahoe vacation home: $40-100k/year revenue
- Palm Springs pool home: $50-120k/year
- Wine country B&B: $40-80k/year
- San Diego beach condo: $30-60k/year
- Bay Area (limited): $20-50k/year (with restrictions)

## Tax treatment

If you don't rent for personal use:
- Treated as rental income (Schedule E)
- Depreciation allowed
- All expenses deductible

If you DO use personally (>14 days or 10% of rental days):
- Mixed-use property rules apply
- Expenses must be allocated
- Personal use days excluded from deductions

## Strategy implications

- Research city regulations BEFORE buying
- Vacation/tourist areas friendlier than urban
- Consider HOA rules in condos
- Build cleaning/management fees into pricing
- Plan for seasonality (high/low season pricing)
- Self-management requires significant time investment
