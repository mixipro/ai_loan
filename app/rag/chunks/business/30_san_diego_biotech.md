# San Diego Biotech Industry

## Industry significance

San Diego is one of TOP 3 US biotech hubs (with Boston, Bay Area):
- 1,300+ biotech companies
- 75,000+ employees
- $50B+ annual revenue
- Strong research universities (UCSD, Scripps, Salk)

## Major biotech clusters

### Torrey Pines Mesa
- Research-focused
- Salk Institute, Scripps Research
- Major pharma R&D centers

### Sorrento Valley
- Mid-size biotech companies
- Genentech, Pfizer presence
- Growing AI-pharma intersection

### Mission Valley/UTC
- Larger commercial operations
- Diagnostic companies

## Notable San Diego biotech companies

Pharmaceutical/biotech leaders:
- **Pfizer** (large San Diego R&D)
- **Illumina** (genome sequencing leader)
- **Genentech** (Roche subsidiary)
- **Qualcomm Life** (health tech)
- **Alphabet Verily**
- **Halozyme Therapeutics**

## Funding landscape

San Diego biotech financing:
- VC funding: $5-10B annually
- IPO market: 10-20 biotech IPOs/year
- M&A: Big pharma acquisitions common
- Major VCs: GV (Google Ventures), ARCH, OrbiMed

## Career opportunities

Biotech roles in San Diego:

### R&D positions
- PhD scientists: $130-200k base
- Postdocs: $55-75k
- Research associates: $70-95k
- Bioinformatics scientists: $130-180k

### Clinical/regulatory
- Clinical research associates: $90-130k
- Regulatory affairs: $120-180k
- Medical affairs: $150-250k

### Commercial
- Product managers: $130-200k
- Marketing managers: $130-180k
- Sales reps: $80-150k + commission

## Equity compensation in biotech

Typical biotech equity:
- Early-stage startups: 0.05-0.5% per employee
- Mid-stage: 0.01-0.1%
- IPO-stage: smaller percentages but higher absolute value
- ISOs common for executive roles
- RSUs at public companies (Illumina, Pfizer)

## Unique biotech finance challenges

### Long timelines
- Drug discovery to market: 10-15 years
- IPO often pre-revenue (clinical trial milestones)
- Equity vests over development cycles
- High failure rate (90%+ drugs fail trials)

### Patent cliffs
- 20-year patent protection
- Generic competition after expiry
- Companies need pipeline replenishment
- Stock prices volatile near patent expiry

### Regulatory risk
- FDA approval not guaranteed
- Single trial failure can destroy company
- Stock down 50%+ on bad clinical data
- Buyer beware on biotech investments

## Tax considerations

For biotech employees in San Diego:
- QSBS for small biotech startups (C-Corp eligible)
- ISO vs RSU tax treatment
- Section 1202 exclusion applies if qualifying
- California state tax adds complexity
- Section 422 for ISO treatment

## Strategy implications

### For biotech founders
- Long fundraising cycles required
- IPO often non-traditional (SPAC, reverse merger options)
- QSBS treatment important for exit planning
- Build for milestones, not revenue

### For biotech employees
- Verify QSBS qualification at hiring
- Don't over-concentrate in employer stock
- Plan for binary outcomes (success or failure)
- Network across companies (consolidation high)

### For biotech investors
- Recognize high failure rates
- Diversify across companies and stages
- Understand clinical trial milestones
- Don't bet single company on regulatory outcome
