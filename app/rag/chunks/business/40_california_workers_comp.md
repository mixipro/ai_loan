# California Workers' Compensation Insurance

## What it is

Workers' compensation insurance covers:
- Medical care for work injuries
- Lost wages during recovery
- Permanent disability benefits
- Death benefits
- Vocational rehabilitation

Required for ALL California employers (with very few exceptions).

## Universal requirement

EVERY California employer needs workers' comp:
- Even 1 part-time employee triggers requirement
- Volunteer organizations (with paid workers)
- Online/remote workers (in California)
- Independent contractors — sometimes (AB 5 implications)

## What employees can claim

Workers' comp covers job-related:
- Physical injuries (accidents, repetitive strain)
- Occupational diseases (asbestos, chemical exposure)
- Cumulative trauma (carpal tunnel, etc.)
- Mental health conditions (in some cases)
- Death benefits for survivors

## Coverage costs

Workers' comp premium rates VARY HUGELY by industry:

### Lowest risk (under 1% of payroll)
- Office workers: 0.4-0.8%
- Clerical: 0.4-0.6%
- Software developers: 0.4-0.8%

### Medium risk (1-3% of payroll)
- Retail: 1.5-3%
- Restaurants: 2-4%
- Light manufacturing: 2-4%

### High risk (5-15% of payroll)
- Construction: 5-15% (varies by trade)
- Roofing: 15-25%
- Trucking: 5-10%
- Logging: 20-30%

## Annual premium examples

For California companies:
- 10-person tech startup (office): $5-10k/year
- 25-person restaurant: $50-100k/year
- 15-person construction crew: $100-300k/year
- 50-person warehouse: $30-100k/year

## Calculating premium

Formula:
- Class code rate × Payroll / $100 = Pure premium
- Multipliers for experience modifier (EMR)
- Additional carrier discounts/loadings

## Experience Modifier (EMR/Mod)

After 3 years, your claims history affects rates:
- **Below 1.0**: Better than industry average (discount)
- **At 1.0**: Industry average
- **Above 1.0**: Worse than average (surcharge)

EMR can swing premiums 30%+ either direction.

## Independent contractor risk

ABC test for IC classification:
- Failing test = employee status required
- Penalties for misclassification: huge
- Workers' comp avoidance = major risk
- AB 5 (2020) makes IC classification harder
- Many former contractors now employees by law

## How to get coverage

California workers' comp options:

### State Compensation Insurance Fund (SCIF)
- Government insurer of last resort
- Accepts everyone
- Often higher rates
- Cumbersome but reliable

### Private insurers
- More carriers competing
- Better service typically
- Discount programs for safe employers
- Rating bureaus competitive

### Self-insurance
- Only large employers
- Requires significant reserves
- More cost-effective for large employers

## Cost-saving strategies

How to lower workers' comp costs:

### Safety programs
- Reduce injury rates
- Improve EMR over time
- Discount programs available
- Some carriers reward training programs

### Classification optimization
- Make sure correctly classified
- Some industries have multiple class codes
- Wrong class code = wrong premium
- Audit/review periodically

### Claims management
- Quick reporting (24-48 hours)
- Return-to-work programs
- Safety training for injured workers
- Avoid disputes when possible

## Common mistakes

1. **Not having coverage** (huge fines, personal liability)
2. **Wrong classification** (overpaying or underpaying)
3. **Misreporting payroll** (audit will catch)
4. **Hiding employees** (1099 fraud)
5. **Delayed claim reporting** (penalty increases)

## Penalties for non-compliance

California enforcement is STRICT:
- **No coverage**: Stop-work order
- **Civil penalty**: $1,500-25,000 per uncovered employee
- **Personal liability**: Owner personally liable for claims
- **Criminal charges**: Possible for willful evasion
- **License suspension**: Professional licenses revoked

## Industry-specific issues

### Construction
- Most regulated industry
- Subcontractor requirements
- Bonding tied to workers' comp
- Multiple class codes possible

### Restaurants
- High injury rate (cuts, burns, falls)
- Heavy turnover affects EMR
- Federal minimum wage rules
- ABC alcohol licenses

### Technology/Office
- Lowest workers' comp burden
- Mental health claims emerging
- Remote work issues new
- Repetitive strain claims

## Strategy implications

### For California employers
- Budget workers' comp realistically
- Don't try to avoid coverage
- Use proper classification
- Implement safety programs
- Maintain good EMR over time

### For startups
- Workers' comp adds significant cost
- Plan for $5-50k/year typical
- Construction startups: budget heavily
- Remote workers still need coverage
- Don't pretend employees are contractors

### For independent contractors/freelancers
- Verify classification
- Consider workers' comp coverage yourself
- Self-employed cannot collect own workers' comp easily
- Health insurance separate consideration
