# Central Valley Agriculture Business

## Industry overview

California Central Valley is the most productive agricultural region in the world:
- 250+ crop varieties produced
- $50B+ annual production value
- Top US producer of: almonds, walnuts, pistachios, dairy, citrus
- 25% of US food supply

## Key crops and economics

### Almonds
- 80% of world supply from California
- $5-10B annual industry
- Long-term investment (5+ year trees)
- Water-intensive (significant cost)

### Dairy
- #1 US dairy producer
- 1,300+ dairies
- Margin pressure from cooperative consolidation
- Some shift toward almond milk substitution

### Wine grapes
- World-class regions: Napa, Sonoma, Central Coast
- Lower margins for table grapes
- High capital investment for vineyard establishment
- Long maturation (3-5 years)

### Pistachios
- Faster-growing than almonds
- Lower water consumption per acre
- Premium pricing for organic
- Strong international market

### Tomatoes (processing)
- Lowest margin commodity
- Volume-dependent
- Mechanized harvesting
- Major processing facilities in valley

## Water — the critical constraint

California water rights determine everything:

### Surface water rights
- Riparian rights (oldest)
- Pre-1914 appropriative rights
- Post-1914 permits
- Determines who gets water in droughts

### Groundwater
- 2014 SGMA (Sustainable Groundwater Management Act)
- Many basins in "critical overdraft"
- New regulations affecting pumping
- Land values affected by water access

### Drought impact
- California in chronic drought conditions
- Crop choices shifting to less water-intensive
- Some land going fallow permanently
- Vineyards drying up in eastern valley

## Land economics

Central Valley farmland:
- Almond orchards: $20-40k per acre
- Vineyards: $15-30k per acre (rising)
- Row crop land: $8-15k per acre
- Cattle ranches: $3-8k per acre

## Migration/labor

Central Valley agriculture depends heavily on labor:
- 800,000+ farmworkers
- 60% undocumented
- H-2A visa program for legal workers
- Labor shortages becoming chronic
- Automation accelerating

## Tax considerations for farms

Agricultural tax benefits:
- Section 175: Soil/conservation expense deduction
- Section 1031: Land exchanges
- Section 263A: Inventory exemption
- Section 175(b): Land clearing
- Federal disaster losses

## Business structures

Common farm structures:
- **LLC**: Most common modern structure
- **Family corporation**: Multi-generational
- **Partnership**: For smaller operations
- **Sole proprietor**: Smallest farms only
- **REIT**: For institutional ownership

## California-specific challenges

Unique to California agriculture:
- Highest minimum wage in US ($16/hr 2024)
- Strict environmental regulations (CEQA)
- Water rights complications
- Air quality requirements (Central Valley worst in US)
- AB 1066 overtime for farmworkers (federal exemption)

## Strategy implications

### For farm investors
- Land + crop tree value (e.g., almond orchards)
- Water rights critical (verify before buying)
- Long-term investments (5-10+ year horizons)
- Climate change exposure (drought, heat)
- Labor cost trajectory

### For agribusiness entrepreneurs
- Value-add processing (more margin than farming)
- Direct-to-consumer brands (better margins)
- Agtech startups serving farmers
- Vertical integration possibilities

### For Central Valley residents
- Agriculture stable but slow-growth
- Land values appreciating slowly
- Best for cash flow, not appreciation
- Look beyond agriculture (logistics growing)
