# California Franchise Tax Deep Dive

## What it is

The franchise tax is California's tax for the PRIVILEGE of doing business in California.
Annual tax separate from income tax.

## $800 minimum applies to

ALL entities:
- **LLCs**: $800 minimum
- **C-Corporations**: $800 minimum (plus 8.84% income tax)
- **S-Corporations**: $800 minimum (plus 1.5% income tax)
- **Limited Partnerships**: $800
- **Limited Liability Partnerships**: $800

## When franchise tax is due

Schedule:
- **First payment**: April 15 of taxable year
- **Quarterly estimated**: April, June, Sept, December (if owing)
- **Annual filing**: 3 months after fiscal year end
- **Late filing penalty**: 5%/month, max 25%
- **Late payment penalty**: 0.5%/month + interest

## First-year LLC exemption

LLCs formed 2021 or later:
- NO $800 franchise tax in first year
- Resumes year 2
- This was COVID-era benefit
- Doesn't apply to corporations

## LLC tax on gross receipts (additional)

For LLCs with gross receipts over $250,000:

| Gross Receipts | Additional Annual Fee |
|----------------|---------------------|
| $250k - $499,999 | $900 |
| $500k - $999,999 | $2,500 |
| $1M - $4,999,999 | $6,000 |
| $5M+ | $11,790 |

This is IN ADDITION to $800 base.

So a $5M revenue LLC pays $800 + $11,790 = $12,590/year MINIMUM.

## Corporate income tax (additional for C-Corps)

C-Corp pays:
- $800 minimum franchise tax
- 8.84% on California net income
- "Greater of" rule applies

S-Corp pays:
- $800 minimum
- 1.5% of California net income
- Net income passes to shareholders

## California-specific deductions

What California allows:
- Standard federal deductions
- Some California-specific (R&D credit)
- Foreign income (with some limits)
- Charitable deductions

What California DOESN'T allow:
- Some federal accelerated depreciation
- Section 179 limits
- Different bonus depreciation rules

## "Doing business" definition

California considers you "doing business" if:
- Organized or commercially domiciled in California
- Sales over $711,538 (2024 threshold)
- Real or tangible property over $71,154 in California
- Compensation paid over $71,154 to California employees

Out-of-state companies often hit California franchise tax.

## Strategies to minimize franchise tax

### Strategy 1: Choose right entity
- Single-owner business: Sole proprietor (no franchise tax)
- Growing business: LLC up to $1M revenue
- High-revenue: Compare LLC fees vs S-Corp tax

### Strategy 2: Income shifting
- S-Corp: Salary vs distribution mix
- Time bonuses/expenses across tax years
- Plan capital gains realization

### Strategy 3: Multi-state planning
- Where is income earned vs received
- Out-of-state operations
- Apportionment formulas

### Strategy 4: Dissolution
- If business inactive, formally dissolve
- Avoid annual $800 minimum
- File final returns

## Common franchise tax mistakes

1. **Forgetting first-year exemption** (LLC missed)
2. **Not filing dormant LLC** (still owe $800)
3. **Missing payment deadlines** (penalties accumulate)
4. **Not understanding gross receipts tax** (LLC owners surprised)
5. **Incorrect classification** (LLC vs S-Corp choice)

## Penalties for non-compliance

California enforcement:
- **Failure to file**: 5%/month up to 25%
- **Failure to pay**: 0.5%/month
- **Substantial understatement**: 20%
- **Personal liability**: For unpaid taxes
- **License revocation**: Possibility

## Strategy implications

### For new entrepreneurs
- Form LLC only when ready to operate
- Take advantage of first-year exemption
- Plan around $800 annual cost
- Don't form for "future business idea"

### For growing businesses
- Track gross receipts for tax planning
- Consider S-Corp at $80-100k+ profit
- Plan multi-state structure as you grow
- Hire CPA for complex situations

### For real estate investors
- LLC per property common practice
- Each LLC owes $800 franchise tax
- Calculate ROI net of tax
- Series LLC NOT recognized in California
