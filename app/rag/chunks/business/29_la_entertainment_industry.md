# Los Angeles Entertainment Industry Finance

## Industry overview

LA dominates global entertainment:
- 70% of US film/TV production
- 200,000+ industry jobs
- $50B+ annual revenue
- Hollywood/Burbank concentration

## Income volatility — defining feature

Entertainment workers face EXTREME income variability:

### Typical career patterns:
- Big year: $200k-$1M+
- Bad year: $20-40k
- Average over 5 years: $60-120k
- Top 5% earn 10x the median

This volatility shapes everything financial.

## Income sources

Entertainment workers earn from multiple streams:

### 1. Per-project pay (W-2 or 1099)
- Daily/weekly union rates
- Sometimes guaranteed weeks
- Often gap periods between projects

### 2. Royalties (long-tail income)
- Music: streaming, sync rights
- Writing: book royalties
- Acting: union residuals

### 3. Residuals (union-mandated)
- Network TV: $40-200/episode reuse
- Streaming: lower but ongoing
- WGA, SAG, DGA negotiate rates

### 4. Streaming revenue (newer category)
- Netflix/Hulu/Amazon original residuals
- Lower than network historically
- Subject to ongoing union negotiation

## Union membership

Major LA unions:
- **SAG-AFTRA**: Actors (~160k members)
- **WGA**: Writers (~13k members)
- **DGA**: Directors (~19k members)
- **IATSE**: Crew (~140k members)
- **Teamsters Local 399**: Drivers, transportation

### Union benefits
- Healthcare (through union plans)
- Pension (separate from personal retirement)
- Minimum scale wages
- Residual payments
- Strike fund protection

## Tax strategies for variable income

### Income averaging concepts
While federal income averaging eliminated, some strategies remain:
- **Defer income** when possible (push 1099s to next year)
- **Accelerate deductions** in high years
- **Bunch expenses** in alternating years
- **Time investments** to alternating years

### Aggressive retirement contributions in good years
- Max 401(k) ($23k)
- SEP-IRA for 1099 income (up to $69k)
- Backdoor Roth ($7k)
- Solo 401(k) for sole proprietors

### Schedule C deductions for self-employed
- Home office
- Equipment (cameras, computers, tools)
- Agent fees (10-15% standard)
- Manager fees (15%)
- Lawyer fees
- Industry events, training

## WGA, SAG-AFTRA strike impact

Recent labor disputes affected income:
- 2023 WGA strike: 5 months, lost wages
- 2023 SAG-AFTRA strike: 4 months
- AI concerns drove negotiations
- Streaming residuals improved
- More strikes likely future

## Streaming revenue evolution

Modern streaming changes economics:
- Lower upfront pay vs network TV
- "All in" deals (no residuals)
- Some "back-end" participation
- Global audience but unclear viewership data
- Union negotiations ongoing

## Strategy implications for entertainment workers

1. **Build cash reserves** for low-income years (12+ months ideal)
2. **Diversify income** beyond performing
3. **Tax planning** more critical than for W-2 workers
4. **Health insurance gap** when between projects
5. **Real estate** in LA expensive for variable income
6. **Plan for retirement** earlier (union pension may be insufficient)
