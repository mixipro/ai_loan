# California Employment Law for Businesses

## Why California employment law matters

California has the STRICTEST employment laws in the US.
Common provisions favor employees over employers.
Major financial implications for business owners.

## Minimum wage

California's 2024 minimum wage:
- **Statewide**: $16/hour
- **Fast food workers (AB 1228)**: $20/hour
- **Some cities higher**: SF $18.07, LA $17.28
- **Healthcare workers**: $25/hour (some categories)

Annual cost-of-living adjustments apply.

## Mandatory employee classifications

### Employees vs Independent Contractors
California uses strict ABC test for classification:
- **A**: Worker free from company control
- **B**: Work outside usual course of business
- **C**: Independently established trade

Failing ANY test = employee status required.

### AB 5 (2020)
Implements ABC test, limiting "gig worker" classification.
Many former contractors must be reclassified as employees.

## Workers' compensation

ALL California employees require workers' comp insurance:
- Mandatory for any employee count
- Cost: 0.5-15% of payroll (varies by industry)
- Construction: highest rates
- Office: lowest rates
- Self-employed (LLC member): can opt in

## Disability insurance

Mandatory California programs:
- **SDI (State Disability Insurance)**: ~1% of wages, both employer and employee
- **Paid Family Leave**: From SDI fund
- Up to 8 weeks paid leave for new parents/family care

## Unemployment insurance

- **UI tax**: 3.4% on first $7,000 of wages
- **ETT (Employment Training Tax)**: 0.1% on first $7,000
- Employer paid only

## Required leave laws

California provides extensive employee leave:

### Sick leave
- 24 hours minimum per year (AB 1041)
- Some cities require more
- Cannot be denied for "no doctor note"

### Paid Family Leave
- Up to 8 weeks
- Up to 70% of wages
- Funded through SDI

### CFRA (California Family Rights Act)
- Up to 12 weeks unpaid job-protected leave
- Birth/adoption/serious illness
- Companies with 5+ employees

### Pregnancy disability leave
- Up to 4 months
- Combined with CFRA can extend to 7 months
- Applies to ALL California employers

### Bereavement leave
- Up to 5 days
- Recently mandated (AB 1949)

## Overtime requirements

California's strict overtime:
- 1.5x pay for hours over 8 per day (federal: only 40/week)
- 1.5x pay for hours 1-8 on 7th day worked
- 2x pay for hours over 12 per day
- 2x pay for hours over 8 on 7th day

Higher cost than federal-only states.

## Meal and rest breaks

Mandatory California break schedule:
- **Meal break**: 30 minutes after 5 hours
- **Rest break**: 10 minutes per 4 hours worked
- **Second meal break**: After 10 hours
- Violations: 1 hour penalty pay per missed break

## Discrimination protections

California FEHA (Fair Employment and Housing Act):
- Stronger than federal Title VII
- Covers more protected classes
- Stricter pregnancy/disability protections
- Companies with 5+ employees

## Misclassification penalties

Massive penalties for misclassifying:
- Back wages owed
- Overtime owed
- Unpaid benefits (SDI, UI)
- Penalties up to $25,000 per violation
- Class action lawsuits common

## Wage theft penalties

California Labor Code 558.1:
- Personal liability for employers (individuals)
- Triple damages possible
- Attorney fees for plaintiffs (asymmetric)
- Severe deterrent

## Strategy implications

### For California small business owners
- Budget 1.4-1.8x salary for true employee cost
- Avoid "1099 contractor" cottage industry mistake
- Comply meticulously with break/meal laws
- Document everything
- Don't pay below California minimum

### For startups
- California much more expensive than other states
- Consider remote-first to access non-CA talent
- Use proper payroll service (Gusto, Paychex, etc.)
- Get employment law consultation early

### Compliance costs estimate
For California 10-person company:
- Workers' comp: $5-50k/year
- HR/Payroll software: $3-10k/year
- SDI/UI taxes: $1-2k/employee
- Legal consultation: $2-10k/year
- Total compliance burden: $50-150k/year
