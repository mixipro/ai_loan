# California LLC Formation Guide

## Why form an LLC

LLC (Limited Liability Company) advantages:
- Personal asset protection from business liability
- Pass-through taxation (no double taxation)
- Flexible management structure
- Less paperwork than corporation
- No required board meetings

## California LLC formation costs

Setting up California LLC:
- **Articles of Organization filing fee**: $70
- **Annual statement filing**: $20
- **First-year tax exemption**: Yes (for LLCs formed 2021+)
- **Annual franchise tax**: $800 (mandatory after year 1)
- **Legal services (optional)**: $500-2,000

Total first-year cost: ~$100-2,000+

## The $800 franchise tax (key concern)

California's $800 minimum franchise tax:
- Required even with $0 revenue
- Required even with first-year exemption (after year 1)
- Cannot be avoided unless LLC dissolved
- Significant burden for new businesses

## Step-by-step formation

### 1. Choose LLC name
- Must be unique in California
- Must end with "LLC" or "Limited Liability Company"
- Check availability: California Secretary of State
- Reserve name (optional): $10/60 days

### 2. Designate registered agent
- Must have California physical address
- Available 9am-5pm business days
- Can be member, friend, or commercial service ($100-300/year)

### 3. File Articles of Organization (Form LLC-1)
- Online or by mail
- $70 filing fee
- 3-5 business days processing
- Online: $20 expedited 24-hour service

### 4. Get EIN from IRS
- Free online (10 minutes)
- Required for tax filings
- Required for business banking

### 5. Open business bank account
- Required for liability protection (separate finances)
- Brings EIN + Articles
- Most major banks support

### 6. Create operating agreement
- Required by California law (LLC-12)
- Internal document, not filed
- Defines management, profits, dissolution
- Template forms available ($0-200)

### 7. Register for taxes
- California state tax: Form 568
- Sales tax permit if applicable
- Local business license

## LLC vs Corporation comparison

| Feature | LLC | C-Corp | S-Corp |
|---------|-----|--------|--------|
| Taxation | Pass-through | Double | Pass-through |
| Owners | Members | Shareholders | 100 max shareholders |
| Equity raise | Limited VC interest | VC standard | Restricted |
| QSBS eligible | NO | YES | NO |
| Self-employment tax | Members pay | No | Salary only |
| Owner restrictions | Few | Few | US citizen/resident |
| California franchise tax | $800 min | $800 min | $800 min |

## When LLC makes sense

GOOD fit:
- Service businesses (consulting, agencies)
- Real estate investments
- Single-owner businesses
- Family/friend partnerships
- Bootstrap-funded ventures

POOR fit:
- VC-funded startups (most VCs require C-Corp)
- Tech startups planning QSBS
- Companies with >100 owners

## California LLC tax structure

By default California LLC is taxed as:
- Single member: Disregarded entity (Schedule C)
- Multi-member: Partnership (1065)
- Can elect S-Corp or C-Corp taxation

## Annual compliance

Required California LLC filings:
- **Statement of Information**: Every 2 years ($20)
- **Form 568 (LLC Return of Income)**: Annual
- **Franchise tax payment**: April 15 ($800 minimum)
- **Estimated tax payments**: Quarterly if profitable

## Maintaining liability protection

Don't pierce the corporate veil:
- Keep separate bank accounts
- Don't commingle personal/business funds
- Use proper documentation
- Maintain proper records
- File annual statements

## Strategy implications

- LLC is good for individual entrepreneurs in CA
- Don't form LLC without business plans (the $800 hurts)
- Real estate investors: LLC offers liability protection
- High-revenue solo: consider S-Corp election
- VC-funded ventures: stick with C-Corp
