# California Business Licensing Requirements

## Why licensing matters

California has THOUSANDS of license requirements.
Missing required licenses leads to:
- Fines and penalties
- Forced business closure
- Personal liability
- Inability to enforce contracts

## Universal requirements for California businesses

### Federal level
- EIN (Employer Identification Number) — free from IRS
- Federal tax registration

### State level
- Seller's Permit (CDTFA) — for selling tangible goods
- Employer registration (EDD) — for employees
- California Secretary of State filings (LLC/Corp)
- State income tax registration

### City/County level
- Business license (almost every city)
- Some require professional certification

## State professional licenses

California regulates 200+ professions:

### Most regulated professions
- **Real estate agents/brokers**: DRE license
- **Insurance agents**: California Department of Insurance
- **Attorneys**: State Bar of California
- **CPAs**: California Board of Accountancy
- **Doctors**: Medical Board of California
- **Contractors**: Contractors State License Board
- **Cosmetologists**: Board of Barbering and Cosmetology

## Special category licenses

### Food service
- Health permit (county-issued)
- Seller's permit (sales tax)
- Liquor license (if applicable)
- Cottage Food Operation (limited home production)

### Cannabis
- State cannabis license (DCC)
- Local cannabis permit
- Track and trace registration
- Background checks

### Construction
- General contractor (B license)
- Specialty contractor (various classes)
- Insurance and bonding requirements
- Workers' comp mandatory

### Financial services
- Money transmitter (DFPI)
- Lender license
- Investment adviser registration
- Securities broker-dealer

## Permits and approvals

Beyond licenses, often need:
- **Zoning permit**: Building/occupancy
- **Health permit**: Food service, certain services
- **Sign permit**: Many cities regulate signage
- **Police permit**: Some businesses (e.g., pawn shops)
- **Fire department**: Inspection required
- **Environmental**: Manufacturing, chemicals
- **CEQA review**: Major construction

## Sales tax basics

If selling tangible goods in California:
- Get Seller's Permit (free)
- Collect 7.25% state sales tax (varies by city)
- Some cities add 1-2.5% (up to ~10% total)
- File returns quarterly typically
- Pay collected tax to state

## Specific city examples

### San Francisco
- Business registration: $99-50k annually (revenue-based)
- Sales tax: 8.625%
- Multiple business permits possible
- Gross receipts tax (large businesses)

### Los Angeles
- Business tax registration: variable
- Sales tax: 9.5%
- Mostly straightforward licensing
- Some industries regulated heavier

### San Diego
- Business tax: minimal
- Sales tax: 7.75%
- Generally pro-business

### Sacramento
- Lower business taxes
- Sales tax: 8.75%
- State capital benefit

## Online tools

California business resources:
- **CalGold**: calgold.ca.gov — find required permits
- **California Business Portal**: business.ca.gov
- **Secretary of State**: bizfile.sos.ca.gov
- **CDTFA**: cdtfa.ca.gov (sales tax)
- **EDD**: edd.ca.gov (employment taxes)

## Costs of compliance

Typical California business licensing costs:
- Initial setup: $200-2,000
- Annual maintenance: $100-1,000+
- Professional licenses: $200-500+ (some industries higher)
- Specialized permits: variable widely
- Late fees and penalties: substantial

## Common compliance mistakes

1. **Operating without local business license** (common for online businesses)
2. **Not collecting sales tax** (very common)
3. **Misclassifying employees vs contractors**
4. **Missing renewal deadlines**
5. **Operating outside zoning rules**
6. **Hiring without I-9 verification**

## Strategy implications

### For new businesses
- Use CalGold to identify ALL required permits BEFORE operating
- Budget for licensing costs (often surprises new entrepreneurs)
- Consider lower-burden states for some operations
- Hire compliance consultant for complex setups
- Don't skip "small" permits — fines accumulate

### For ongoing operations
- Track all license renewal dates
- Monitor regulatory changes
- Annual compliance review
- Professional advisor relationships
- Don't ignore notices from agencies
