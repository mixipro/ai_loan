# California Minimum Wage Landscape

## Statewide minimum wage 2024

California statewide: $16/hour
- Applies to all employees of all sizes
- Annual cost-of-living adjustments
- Adjusts each January 1

## Industry-specific minimums (higher)

### Fast Food workers (AB 1228, effective April 2024)
- **$20/hour** minimum
- Applies to fast food chains with 60+ US locations
- McDonald's, Burger King, Subway, etc.

### Healthcare workers (SB 525)
- $25/hour for large healthcare facilities
- Lower rates for smaller facilities
- Phased implementation

### Garment industry (SB 62)
- Piece-rate prohibition
- Hourly minimum required
- Higher enforcement

## Tipped employee rules

California is RARE state with NO tip credit:
- Tipped employees earn FULL minimum wage
- Tips on top, not in lieu of wages
- Tip pooling regulated
- This significantly impacts restaurant economics

## Special category exemptions

Some workers may earn below minimum:
- Outside salespeople (commission-based)
- Higher-level executives
- Some apprentices (limited)
- Family member exceptions (small operations)
- Many exemptions are narrow and contested

## Cost impact for businesses

### Hidden costs of minimum wage
- **Direct wage cost**: Per hour increase
- **Workers' comp**: Often percentage of wages
- **SDI/UI taxes**: Increase with wages
- **Overtime base**: Higher base = higher overtime
- **Total cost per employee**: 1.3-1.5x direct wage

### Example: 10-employee retail in LA
- Statewide minimum: $16 × 2,000 hrs × 10 = $320k/year
- LA city minimum: $17.28 × 2,000 hrs × 10 = $345k/year
- **Annual difference**: $25k just from city wage

## Strategy implications

### For employers in California
- **Don't pay minimum** (attracts higher turnover, harder to retain)
- **Pay $2-5 above minimum** for retention
- **Use technology** to reduce labor needs
- **Outsource non-core** when economically sensible
- **Consider remote** for non-California talent

### Industry-specific impact
- **Restaurants**: Most affected (high labor, low margins)
- **Retail**: High impact (especially e-commerce alternatives)
- **Hospitality**: Major margins squeeze
- **Manufacturing**: Less affected (higher wages already)
- **Construction**: Union scales typically much higher

### For startups
- California labor 50%+ more than Texas, Florida, etc.
- Remote work strategies critical
- Consider operating outside California for some functions
- Headquarters California for talent, ops elsewhere

## Future minimum wage trajectory

Likely future increases:
- Annual ~3-5% cost-of-living adjustments
- Industry-specific raises (healthcare, fast food)
- Some cities pushing $25+ targets
- Federal indexing proposals
- $30/hour Bay Area discussion ongoing
