# California Investor Protections and Regulations

## State-level oversight

California provides additional investor protection beyond federal SEC:
- **DFPI** (Department of Financial Protection and Innovation)
- **California Corporate Securities Law**
- **California Consumer Financial Protection Law**

## DFPI authority

DFPI regulates:
- Investment advisers in California
- Broker-dealers and agents
- Crowdfunding offerings
- California-based investment funds
- Money transmitters and lenders

## California-specific investor rights

### Limited offering exemptions (CA Corp Code 25102(f))
California allows certain limited offerings to accredited investors:
- 35 or fewer purchasers
- Notice filing required
- Investor disclosures mandatory

### California "blue sky" laws
State-level securities registration requirements:
- Stricter than federal in some cases
- Apply to in-state offerings
- Foreign offerings still need state notice

## Anti-fraud provisions

California has STRONGER anti-fraud rules than federal:
- Material misrepresentation = civil liability
- Rescission rights for victims
- Punitive damages available
- 5-year statute of limitations (longer than federal)

## Investor recovery options

If defrauded in California:
1. **SEC complaint** (federal level)
2. **DFPI complaint** (state level)
3. **FINRA arbitration** (broker disputes)
4. **Securities class action** (group recovery)
5. **California civil court** (individual lawsuit)

## Crowdfunding regulations

California is friendlier to investment crowdfunding than most states:
- Reg CF (federal) plus California rules
- Up to $5M for in-state offerings
- Required disclosure documents
- Investor limits based on income/net worth

## Real estate investment regulations

California strict on:
- TIC (Tenants in Common) offerings
- DST (Delaware Statutory Trust) sales
- Promotion of real estate funds
- Disclosure requirements heavier than federal

## Accredited investor definition

To qualify for unregistered offerings:
- Income $200k+ ($300k joint) for 2 years
- Net worth $1M+ (excluding primary residence)
- Or professional license (Series 7, 65, 82)

California uses federal definition but enforces stricter.

## Investor advisor regulations

California-based investment advisers:
- Must register with state if under $100M AUM
- Federal registration if $100M+
- Fiduciary duty stricter than broker standard
- Annual filings required

## Common California investment scams

DFPI warnings about:
- "California-specific" real estate schemes
- Crypto investment scams targeting California residents
- Affinity fraud (targeting specific communities)
- "Hard money lending" Ponzi schemes
- Pre-IPO stock offering scams

## Protections for retirement accounts

California offers ADDITIONAL protection:
- IRAs largely protected from creditors
- 401(k) ERISA protection (federal)
- California allows up to $1.5M in IRA protection in bankruptcy
- Strong against most lawsuits

## Investor due diligence

For California investors, always:
1. Check adviser registration (DFPI website)
2. Verify firm licensing (FINRA BrokerCheck)
3. Read offering documents carefully
4. Verify accredited investor status correctly
5. Document all communications
6. Be wary of pressure tactics

## Strategy implications

- Use registered, licensed professionals only
- Verify all claims independently
- California has strong investor protections — use them
- Take advantage of state-level remedies when needed
- Keep documentation of all investment decisions
