# California-Specific ETFs and Mutual Funds

## Why California-specific funds

For California residents, certain funds offer:
- State tax exemption on dividends
- Exposure to California economy
- Tax-loss harvesting opportunities

## California Municipal Bond Funds

These funds hold California muni bonds (double tax-free for residents):

### Vanguard
- **VCITX**: California Long-Term Tax-Exempt
- **VCAIX**: California Intermediate-Term Tax-Exempt  
- **VCADX**: California Tax-Exempt Money Market

### Fidelity
- **FCTFX**: California Limited Term Tax-Free Bond
- **FCXAX**: California Municipal Income

### BlackRock/iShares
- **CMF**: iShares California Muni Bond ETF
- **CXA**: SPDR Nuveen Bloomberg California Municipal Bond

## Yield comparison

California muni vs taxable bonds (for 50% bracket Californian):

| Investment | Pre-tax Yield | After-tax Yield |
|------------|---------------|-----------------|
| US Treasury 10-year | 4.3% | 4.3% × (1-0.37) = 2.71% (state exempt) |
| Corporate bond fund | 5.5% | 5.5% × (1-0.50) = 2.75% |
| **VCAIX** (CA muni) | **3.2%** | **3.2% (double tax-free)** |

CA muni bond wins on after-tax basis.

## Concentration risk warning

California tech workers face DOUBLE concentration:
1. Salary depends on tech industry
2. Employer stock (RSUs/ISOs) likely tech
3. Adding more tech ETFs compounds risk

Better diversification:
- Broad market (VTI, VOO) instead of tech-specific
- International exposure (VXUS)
- Bonds and alternatives

## Tax-loss harvesting opportunities

California muni funds:
- Can offset other capital gains
- Pair with broader index fund (sell loss, buy similar)
- Substantial tax savings for high-bracket Californians

## Recommended California allocation

For typical California tech worker portfolio:
- 60-70%: Broad index (VTI/VOO)
- 15-20%: International (VXUS)
- 10-15%: Bonds (CA muni for taxable account)
- 5%: Real estate (VNQ)

NOT recommended:
- Heavy California tech ETF on top of tech RSUs
- High-yield CA muni (default risk for marginal yield)
