# Employee Stock Purchase Plan (ESPP) Strategies

## What ESPP is

Company benefit allowing employees to buy company stock at a discount through payroll deductions.
Common at most public tech companies.

## Standard ESPP terms

Typical 423(b) qualified ESPP:
- **Discount**: 10-15% off market price
- **Offering period**: 6-12 months
- **Purchase periods**: Often 6 months
- **Lookback feature**: Compare price at start vs end of period
- **Maximum contribution**: $25,000 per year (IRS limit)
- **Maximum shares per period**: Often capped

## The "lookback" magic

The lookback provision is the most valuable ESPP feature:
- Compares stock price at OFFERING START vs PURCHASE DATE
- Uses LOWER price for the 15% discount
- Effective discount can be much more than 15%

Example with lookback:
- Stock at offering start: $100
- Stock at purchase: $150
- Without lookback: 15% off $150 = $127.50/share (15% effective return)
- With lookback: 15% off $100 = $85/share (43% effective return!)

## Tax treatment

ESPP tax depends on disposition timing:

### Qualifying disposition
Hold shares 2 years from OFFERING DATE and 1 year from PURCHASE DATE:
- Discount taxed as ordinary income (capped at 15% or actual gain)
- Additional gain taxed as long-term capital gains
- Best tax outcome

### Disqualifying disposition
Sell earlier:
- Full discount (difference between FMV at purchase and purchase price) taxed as ordinary income
- Additional gain or loss taxed as capital gain/loss

## ESPP strategies

### Strategy 1: Sell immediately (most common)
- Sell shares as soon as purchased
- Lock in 15-25% effective discount as guaranteed return
- Reduces concentration risk
- Disqualifying disposition (ordinary income on discount)
- Best for risk-averse employees

### Strategy 2: Hold for qualifying disposition
- Hold shares 2+ years from offering, 1+ from purchase
- Better tax treatment on appreciation
- More risk (stock could decline)
- Worth it only if confident in stock

### Strategy 3: Max contribution + immediate sell
- Contribute maximum allowed ($25k/year)
- Sell every purchase period
- Reinvest in diversified portfolio
- Effectively converts salary into investment with 15-25% bonus

## Why ESPP is usually worth maxing

For most California tech workers:
- 15% guaranteed return on contribution amount
- Better than most "safe" investments
- Lookback can double effective return
- Forced savings mechanism

## Common mistakes

1. **Not participating** when offered (leaves money on table)
2. **Not maxing contribution** when cash flow allows
3. **Holding too long** without diversification plan
4. **Forgetting tax implications** of disqualifying disposition
5. **Not understanding lookback** (huge value)

## California considerations

California taxes ESPP discount as ordinary income (same as federal).
No state preferential treatment.
But still highly worth participating.

## Liquidity considerations

- Money locked up during offering period (paycheck deductions)
- Need sufficient regular paycheck cushion
- Plan for 6-month delay before realizing benefit
