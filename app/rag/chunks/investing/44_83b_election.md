# 83(b) Election — Critical for Founders and Early Employees

## What it is

An election to pay taxes NOW on unvested stock at current (low) value,
instead of when it vests at higher value.

## When it matters most

Most powerful for FOUNDERS receiving founder stock:
- Stock issued at $0.001/share value (essentially free)
- Becomes valuable as company grows
- Without 83(b): tax owed at vest on potentially massive appreciation
- With 83(b): tax owed now on minimal value

## The math

Founder gets 1M shares at $0.001/share grant value:

WITHOUT 83(b):
- Year 4 vesting: shares worth $1/share = $1,000,000 value
- Taxed as ordinary income (37% federal + 13.3% CA = 50%)
- **Tax bill: $500,000**

WITH 83(b):
- Pay tax NOW on $1,000 spread (1M × $0.001)
- Tax bill: ~$500 (negligible)
- Save $499,500 in taxes

## CRITICAL DEADLINE

Must file within 30 DAYS of grant. NO EXCEPTIONS.
Missing the deadline = lose the option permanently.

## How to file

1. Complete IRS Form 83(b)
2. Send to IRS via certified mail within 30 days
3. Give copy to employer (HR/legal)
4. Keep copy for your records
5. Attach copy to tax return that year

## Risks of 83(b)

The election has risks:

### Risk 1: Leave company before vesting
- If you leave, you lose the stock AND the tax you paid
- No refund possible
- Pay tax on theoretical value that you never realized

### Risk 2: Stock goes to zero
- Pay tax on value that disappears
- No refund possible
- Capital loss only when actually sold

### Risk 3: Unable to file timely
- Strict 30-day rule
- No extensions, no exceptions
- Tax court has rejected late filings

## When NOT to file 83(b)

DON'T elect if:
- Vesting schedule is short (1 year or less)
- Company already late-stage (high stock value, big tax bill now)
- High probability of leaving before vesting
- Cash flow concern (current tax bill burdensome)

## When to definitely file

DO elect if:
- Founder stock at company formation (huge upside)
- Very early employee (Series A or earlier)
- Stock currently worth pennies
- Long vesting schedule with high upside

## California considerations

California honors 83(b) elections.
But California taxes higher than federal:
- Pay both fed and CA tax on ordinary income amount
- Even with 83(b), can have decent tax bill
- Important to plan cash flow for tax payment

## Best practices

1. **Default to filing 83(b)** for founder/very early employee stock
2. **Get legal/tax advice** for specific situation
3. **File via certified mail** with return receipt
4. **Document everything** (proof of mailing is critical)
5. **Set calendar reminder** for tax return attachment
