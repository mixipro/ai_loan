# ISO vs NSO — Tax Treatment Compared

## Quick comparison

| Feature | ISO | NSO |
|---------|-----|-----|
| Who can receive | Employees only | Anyone |
| Tax at grant | None | None |
| Tax at exercise | AMT may apply | Ordinary income (bargain element) |
| Tax at sale (qualifying) | Long-term capital gains | Long-term capital gains on appreciation |
| Tax at sale (disqualifying) | Ordinary + capital | Capital gains on appreciation |
| FICA at exercise | No | Yes |
| Maximum value | $100k/year ISO | No limit |
| Holding period | 2y from grant + 1y from exercise | 1 year for LTCG |

## ISO tax mechanics

When you EXERCISE an ISO:
- Pay strike price
- Receive shares
- NO regular income tax at exercise
- BUT: bargain element (FMV minus strike) counts toward AMT

When you SELL ISO shares:
- **Qualifying disposition** (held 2y from grant + 1y from exercise):
  - All gain treated as long-term capital gains
  - Best tax outcome
- **Disqualifying disposition** (sold earlier):
  - Bargain element taxed as ordinary income
  - Additional gain as short or long-term capital

## NSO tax mechanics

When you EXERCISE an NSO:
- Pay strike price
- Receive shares  
- Bargain element (FMV minus strike) is ORDINARY INCOME
- Company withholds taxes
- FICA + Medicare apply

When you SELL NSO shares:
- Cost basis = strike price + bargain element already taxed
- Only appreciation since exercise is capital gain
- Short-term or long-term based on hold from exercise date

## AMT trap for ISOs

If you exercise ISOs and hold:
- Bargain element counts toward AMT calculation
- May owe AMT in year of exercise even though no cash received
- Example: Exercise $50k worth of ISO bargain element
  - Regular tax: $0
  - AMT: ~$8,000-12,000 owed
- Get partial credit in future years if you don't sell

## Strategic considerations

### When ISOs are better
- Long-term hold planned (qualifies for LTCG)
- AMT impact is manageable
- Low income year for exercise

### When NSOs are simpler
- Will sell soon after exercise
- Don't want AMT complexity
- Late-stage company (less appreciation potential)

## California specifics

California does NOT recognize ISO favorable treatment fully:
- California treats ISOs more like NSOs for state tax
- Bargain element taxed as ordinary income for CA
- Federal preferential rates apply, state does not
- Higher effective tax for California ISO holders
