# Mega Backdoor Roth — Powerful Strategy for California Tech Workers

## What it is

A way to contribute UP TO $46,000 EXTRA per year to a Roth account
beyond standard limits — entirely through your 401(k).

Particularly powerful for high-earning California tech workers with employer plans.

## The basics

Standard 401(k) limits 2024:
- Employee deferral: $23,000 (under 50)
- Total employee + employer + after-tax: $69,000

The "extra space" between these is mega backdoor opportunity:
- $69,000 - $23,000 (your contribution) - $X,XXX (employer match) = remaining space
- Fill this with AFTER-TAX contributions
- Convert immediately to Roth = tax-free growth

## How it works

Step 1: Make after-tax 401(k) contributions (not Roth, not traditional)
Step 2: Convert immediately to Roth IRA or Roth 401(k)
Step 3: All future growth is tax-free

## Requirements

Your employer's 401(k) must support:
- After-tax (NOT Roth) contributions
- In-service conversion or in-plan rollover to Roth
- Or distributions while still employed

Common at big tech companies:
- Google, Meta, Apple, Microsoft, Amazon: Yes
- Many startups: Sometimes
- Smaller employers: Often no

## Real math example

Software engineer at FAANG, 2024:
- Salary: $300,000
- Employee 401(k): $23,000
- Employer match: $11,500 (3.83%)
- Available after-tax space: $69,000 - $23,000 - $11,500 = $34,500
- Convert $34,500 to Roth annually
- Over 30 years at 7% growth: **$3.2M+ tax-free**

## California state tax angle

California state tax matters:
- After-tax contribution: Already taxed at California rates
- No additional California tax on conversion
- Tax-free in retirement (federal AND state)
- HUGE benefit for California residents

Without mega backdoor:
- After-tax investments grow taxed each year
- California taxes dividends as ordinary income
- 1-2% drag annually from taxes

With mega backdoor Roth:
- $0 California tax on growth
- $0 California tax on withdrawals in retirement
- Effectively gets 1-2% extra return per year

## Strategy considerations

### When mega backdoor makes sense
- High income (in top tax brackets)
- Employer 401(k) supports after-tax + in-service conversion
- Already maxing other tax-advantaged accounts
- Long time horizon (more time for tax-free growth)
- Adequate emergency fund and short-term savings

### When to skip
- Low employer 401(k) match (less benefit)
- Need cash for housing down payment soon
- Plan limitations make rollover complex
- Lower tax bracket

## Coordination with other accounts

Maximize tax efficiency in order:
1. Max 401(k) employer match (free money)
2. Max HSA if eligible ($4,150 single, $8,300 family)
3. Max Roth IRA backdoor ($7,000)
4. Max 401(k) employee deferral ($23,000)
5. Mega Backdoor Roth (remaining space)
6. Taxable brokerage account (everything else)

## California early retirees

California tech workers often retire early. Mega backdoor especially powerful:
- Roth contributions accessible at 59.5
- Conversions accessible 5 years after each conversion
- Plan distribution sequence carefully
- Can fund early retirement before traditional retirement age

## Common mistakes

1. **Not understanding employer plan** (some don't support it)
2. **Not converting promptly** (after-tax growth becomes taxable)
3. **Missing in-service conversion option**
4. **Confusing with regular backdoor Roth** (different strategies)
5. **Triggering pro-rata rule** (less of an issue with mega backdoor)
