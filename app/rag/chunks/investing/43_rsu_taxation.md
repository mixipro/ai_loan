# RSU Taxation Deep Dive

## What RSUs are

Restricted Stock Units (RSUs) are PROMISES to deliver shares upon vesting.
Most common at public tech companies (FAANG, large tech).

## The two taxable events

### Event 1: Vesting
At vest, you receive shares (or cash value):
- Treated as ordinary income at FAIR MARKET VALUE on vest date
- Company withholds federal + state + FICA + Medicare
- Reported on W-2 (Box 14 typically shows "RSU")
- California state tax: up to 13.3%

### Event 2: Sale (later)
When you sell vested shares:
- Cost basis = FMV at vest (already taxed)
- Gain/loss measured from FMV at vest
- Short-term (< 1 year hold) vs Long-term (> 1 year hold)

## Sell-to-cover vs cash withholding

Companies handle withholding two ways:

### Sell-to-cover (most common)
- Company sells portion of vesting shares to cover tax
- You receive net shares
- Tax withholding rate: usually 22-37% federal (supplemental rate)
- May not cover full tax liability for high earners

### Cash withholding
- You pay tax in cash from other sources
- Receive all vested shares
- Better if you want to maximize shares

## The "tax bill bomb" problem

Big risk: stock drops after vesting but BEFORE you can sell.

Example:
- 100 RSUs vest at $200 = $20,000 income (ordinary tax)
- Federal tax (35%): $7,000
- California tax (10%): $2,000
- Total tax: $9,000
- Stock drops to $100 by tax day
- You owe $9,000 but shares only worth $10,000
- Net: 10% of original value left after taxes

## Strategy: Sell on vest day

Most financial advisors recommend SELLING RSUs immediately at vest:
- You're already taxed at FMV
- Selling has no additional tax impact
- Reduces concentration risk
- Diversify proceeds into broader portfolio

## Hold for long-term capital gains?

Holding RSUs past vest can be smart IF:
- High conviction in company stock
- Already diversified elsewhere
- Stock has dropped (could be near-term opportunity)
- Year-end tax-loss harvesting opportunity

## Tax withholding insufficient?

For high earners, RSU withholding (22% federal default) is often INSUFFICIENT:
- Real marginal rate: 37%+ federal
- 15% gap = surprise tax bill
- Solution: Make quarterly estimated payments
- Or sell more shares to cover

## California-specific notes

- RSU vesting income subject to California source rules
- If you vest in California then leave, may still owe CA tax
- 6-7 year California "tail" on some equity income
- Plan moves carefully around vest schedules
