# Equity Compensation Types Explained

## Overview

California tech workers commonly receive 4 types of equity compensation.
Each has different tax treatment and strategic considerations.

## ISO (Incentive Stock Options)

- Available ONLY to employees (not contractors)
- Bargain element NOT taxable at exercise (regular tax)
- BUT subject to AMT (Alternative Minimum Tax) at exercise
- 2-year/1-year holding requirements for long-term capital gains
- Best for low-income exercise years
- Maximum $100k vesting per year qualifies as ISO

## NSO (Non-qualified Stock Options)

- Available to anyone (employees, advisors, contractors)
- Bargain element IS taxable at exercise (ordinary income)
- Company withholds taxes automatically
- Cleaner tax treatment, no AMT issues
- Common for late-stage employees and consultants

## RSU (Restricted Stock Units)

- Most common at public tech companies (Google, Meta, Apple)
- Taxed as ORDINARY INCOME at vesting (not at sale)
- Company withholds shares for taxes ("sell to cover")
- Subject to FICA + Medicare at vest
- California state tax: up to 13.3% on vest value

## ESPP (Employee Stock Purchase Plan)

- Typically 10-15% discount on company stock
- Lookback feature can boost effective discount to 25%+
- 6-month purchase periods common
- Qualifying disposition: 2 years from offering, 1 year from purchase
- Disqualifying disposition: shorter hold, ordinary income on discount

## Strategy for California tech workers

1. **Don't hold more than 10% net worth in employer stock** (concentration risk)
2. **Diversify post-vest** — sell some RSUs to fund other investments
3. **AMT planning for ISOs** — model tax impact before exercise
4. **ESPP is usually worth maxing** (free 10-25% return)
5. **Consider QSBS qualification** for ISOs in qualified startups

## Common mistakes

- Holding all employer stock (Enron-style risk)
- Missing ISO holding periods (loses preferential rates)
- Not modeling AMT impact before exercise
- Selling at wrong time tax-wise
- Ignoring vesting cliff dates
