# Tax-Loss Harvesting for California Investors

## What it is

Tax-loss harvesting = selling investments at a loss to offset capital gains
and reduce tax liability, while maintaining similar market exposure.

## Why it matters more in California

California's high state tax rates make tax-loss harvesting MORE valuable:
- Federal LTCG rate: 20%
- California rate: up to 13.3%
- NIIT: 3.8%
- **Combined max: 37.1%**

Saving 37.1% on $10k loss harvested = $3,710 saved.

## How it works

1. Sell investment showing a loss
2. Use loss to offset gains (or up to $3k of ordinary income)
3. IMMEDIATELY buy similar (but not identical) investment
4. Maintain market exposure while capturing tax benefit

## The wash sale rule

IRS Section 1091 — can't claim loss if you buy "substantially identical" security within 30 days before or after sale.

Wash sale violations:
- Selling VOO and buying VOO within 31 days (same ETF)
- Selling Apple and buying Apple call options
- Spouse buying the same security in their account

Wash sale safe:
- Selling VOO and buying VTI (similar but different)
- Selling VOO and buying SCHX (different fund family)
- Selling Apple and buying QQQ (related but not identical)

## Common pairs for tax-loss harvesting

These pairs are SIMILAR but not "substantially identical":
- VOO ↔ SPY ↔ IVV (S&P 500 funds, different issuers)
- VTI ↔ ITOT (total market, different issuers)
- VXUS ↔ IXUS (international, different issuers)
- BND ↔ AGG (total bond market, different issuers)
- VEA ↔ IEFA (developed international, different)

## California-specific opportunities

### California muni bonds + national muni
Sell California muni at loss, buy national muni (still tax-exempt):
- Maintain bond exposure
- Slightly different state tax treatment
- Not "substantially identical"

### Tech stock concentration
California tech workers often have RSU losses to harvest:
- Sell vested RSU shares at loss (if down)
- Buy similar tech ETF (XLK, QQQ)
- Reduce single-stock risk + harvest loss

## Maximum loss deduction

- Capital losses fully offset capital gains
- Net loss up to $3,000 against ordinary income per year
- Excess carries forward indefinitely
- Married filing separately: $1,500 limit

## Best practices

### When to harvest
- **Year-end review** (December)
- **Major market drops** (capture losses early)
- **Before realizing gains** (offset planned sales)
- **High income year** (maximum tax benefit)

### What to avoid
- Wash sale violations (lose the loss)
- Tax-tail wagging investment dog (don't sell good investments just for tax)
- IRA wash sales (no benefit, complications)
- Spouse buying same security (counts as wash sale)

## Maximum tax savings example

California tech worker with $50,000 in losses:
- Offset $50,000 in RSU gains
- Federal savings (20%): $10,000
- California savings (13.3%): $6,650
- NIIT savings (3.8%): $1,900
- **Total saved: $18,550**

That's real money in your pocket.
