# HSA — Special California Tax Treatment

## What HSA is

Health Savings Account — triple tax-advantaged account for medical expenses:
1. Tax-deductible contributions (federal)
2. Tax-free growth
3. Tax-free withdrawals for qualified medical expenses

Often called "stealth IRA" because of unique flexibility.

## 2024 contribution limits

- Single coverage: $4,150
- Family coverage: $8,300
- Catch-up (55+): $1,000 additional
- Must have High Deductible Health Plan (HDHP)

## CRITICAL California quirk

California does NOT conform to federal HSA tax treatment:
- **California taxes HSA contributions as INCOME**
- **California taxes HSA investment growth annually**
- Only federal benefit applies in California

This is a major disadvantage compared to other states.

## What this means in practice

For a Bay Area tech worker in highest brackets:

### Federal level (favorable)
- Contribute $8,300 family
- Federal income tax saved (37%): $3,071
- No federal tax on growth
- No federal tax on qualified withdrawal

### California level (unfavorable)
- $8,300 contribution adds to California taxable income
- California tax on growth (each year)
- Effective California tax (13.3%): $1,104 paid

### Net result
- Federal save: $3,071
- California cost: $1,104
- **Net benefit: $1,967** (still positive, but less than other states)

## Is HSA still worth it in California?

YES, generally:
- Federal benefit usually exceeds California cost
- Triple federal benefit remains valuable
- Investment growth tax-free federally (HUGE long-term)
- Use as additional retirement account

NO, considering:
- Already maxing other tax-advantaged accounts
- Low federal bracket (smaller federal save)
- Don't have HDHP (can't contribute)

## Strategic uses

### Strategy 1: Use for current medical expenses
Simple use — pay current bills, get federal deduction.

### Strategy 2: "Save and grow" — best long-term
- Pay medical out-of-pocket NOW (keep receipts)
- Let HSA grow tax-free for decades
- Reimburse yourself anytime in future
- After 65: any withdrawal is taxed like IRA (no penalty)

### Strategy 3: Last expense fund
Pay medical expenses early retirement before Medicare.

## California reporting complexity

HSA in California requires extra tax tracking:
- Form 540 California return adjustments
- Track basis in HSA investments
- Report annual gains as California income
- Different rules for distributions vs federal

Often requires CPA assistance for California residents.

- **Avoid**: HSAs with maintenance fees

## California vs other states

If you're moving:
- HSA still valuable in CA (just less so)
- States like Texas, Nevada conform to federal (better)
- Plan accordingly if state move possible
- HSA travels with you when you move

## Common mistakes in California

1. **Not contributing because of California treatment** (still federal benefit)
2. **Spending HSA early** (loses long-term growth)
3. **Not tracking California basis** (tax complications)
4. **Withdrawing before 65 for non-medical** (penalty + tax)
5. **Missing receipts for future reimbursement** (lose strategy benefit)
