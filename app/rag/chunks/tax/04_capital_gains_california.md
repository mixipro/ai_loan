# Capital Gains in California

## Federal treatment

Federal capital gains are taxed at preferential rates:
- Long-term (held >1 year): 0%, 15%, or 20% based on income
- Short-term (held ≤1 year): ordinary income rates (10%-37%)
- Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT): additional 3.8% over $200k single

## California treatment — KEY DIFFERENCE

California does NOT distinguish between short-term and long-term capital gains.
ALL capital gains are taxed as ORDINARY INCOME (up to 13.3% state).

This makes California one of the worst states for capital gains:
- Federal LTCG: 20%
- California: 13.3%
- NIIT: 3.8%
- **Combined max: 37.1%**

## Real estate capital gains

Section 121 Exclusion (federal AND California honor this):
- $250,000 exclusion for single filers
- $500,000 exclusion for married filing jointly
- Must have lived in home 2 of last 5 years as primary residence
- Once every 2 years

## Strategies for California residents

1. **Hold for federal LTCG**: Wait >1 year for federal benefit even if CA doesn't help
2. **Timing of sale**: If considering moving out of state, sell AFTER move
3. **1031 Exchange**: Defer all gains (federal + state) on investment real estate
4. **Charitable Remainder Trust**: Spread gain recognition over years
5. **Opportunity Zones**: Defer + reduce gains
6. **QSBS for startup equity**: Federal exclusion up to $10M

## Common mistake

Many California residents think long-term gains get state preferential treatment.
They don't. Plan accordingly.
