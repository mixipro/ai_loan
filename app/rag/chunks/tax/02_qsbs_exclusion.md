# QSBS — Qualified Small Business Stock Exclusion

## What it is

Section 1202 of the IRS code allows up to $10 MILLION in tax-free gains 
when selling qualified startup stock. Major benefit for Bay Area tech workers.

## Qualifying conditions

- C-Corporation structure (LLC and S-Corp do NOT qualify)
- Founded after August 10, 1993 (most modern startups qualify)
- Less than $50M in gross assets at issuance
- Active business (not investing/holding company)
- Holding period: 5+ years before sale
- Original issuance stock (not secondary purchase)

## Stack benefit

QSBS exclusion is PER ISSUER, not per person. If you hold stock in 3 different 
qualifying startups, you get $10M exclusion EACH = up to $30M tax-free.

## California state treatment

Unlike federal exclusion, California does NOT conform to QSBS exclusion. 
California taxes the gain at ordinary income rates (up to 13.3%).
However, federal exclusion still saves 20-23% federal capital gains.

## Real example

Engineer joins startup at Series A in 2021, gets 0.1% equity.
Company exits for $1B in 2027.

- Engineer's gain: $1,000,000
- WITHOUT QSBS: federal tax (20%) + CA tax (13.3%) = $333,000
- WITH QSBS: federal $0, CA $133,000 = $133,000
- Savings: $200,000

## Common mistake

Many tech workers don't realize their RSUs from Series A startup qualify for QSBS.
Talk to CPA when joining startup. File Form 1202 documentation.
