# Proposition 13 — California Property Tax Basics

## Core mechanics

Proposition 13 (passed June 6, 1978) caps California property tax at:
- **Base rate: 1% of purchase price** (assessed value)
- **Annual increase: max 2%** regardless of market appreciation
- **Reassessment trigger: sale of property** (with some exceptions)

## Long-term financial impact

Example: $300,000 home bought in 1990:
- Year 1 (1990): Tax = $3,000 (1% of $300k)
- Year 33 (2023): Tax ≈ $5,815 (only +2%/year compounding)
- New buyer of same property (now worth $1.4M): Tax = $14,000

Annual savings for original owner vs new buyer: ~$8,185 per year.

## Key implications for buyers

1. **Lock-in effect**: Buying now locks property tax baseline for life
2. **Long-term holding rewarded**: Older owners pay disproportionately less
3. **Tax doesn't follow market**: Even if home appreciates 5x, tax grows only 2%/year

## Exceptions to reassessment

- Spousal inheritance (no reassessment)
- Parent-child transfer (modified by Prop 19, 2020)
- Renovation under $50k threshold typically doesn't trigger

## Strategy implications

For California real estate buyers in 2026:
- Earlier purchase = bigger lifetime Prop 13 benefit
- High-priced areas (SF, Palo Alto) give biggest absolute dollar savings
- Hold property long-term to maximize Prop 13 advantage
