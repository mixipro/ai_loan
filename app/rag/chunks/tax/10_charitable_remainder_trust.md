# Charitable Remainder Trusts (CRT) for High-Net-Worth Californians

## What it is

A Charitable Remainder Trust is an irrevocable trust that:
1. Pays income to you (or beneficiaries) for life or fixed term
2. Donates the REMAINDER to charity at end
3. Provides immediate tax deduction + capital gains deferral

Powerful tool for California high earners facing huge tax bills.

## Two main types

### CRAT (Charitable Remainder Annuity Trust)
- Fixed annual payment (e.g., $50,000/year)
- Set at creation, doesn't change with trust value
- Better when interest rates are low

### CRUT (Charitable Remainder Unitrust)
- Variable payment based on % of trust value (e.g., 5% of assets)
- Payments grow if trust grows
- Better in growing markets

## How it solves California's tax problem

Scenario: Tech executive selling $5M in startup stock (cost basis $100k).

WITHOUT CRT:
- Gain: $4.9M
- Federal tax (20%): $980k
- California tax (13.3%): $651k
- NIIT (3.8%): $186k
- **Total taxes: $1.8M (37%)**
- Net to executive: $3.2M

WITH CRT:
- Contribute $5M stock to CRT
- Trust sells inside (no immediate capital gains)
- Executive gets:
  - Income tax deduction: ~$1.5M-2M (present value of remainder)
  - Income payments: $250k/year for life
  - Capital gains spread over many years
- Charity gets remainder at end

## Best for

- One-time large liquidity events (startup exits, real estate sales)
- High California state income tax exposure
- Desire to support charitable causes
- Income smoothing needs

## Drawbacks

- IRREVOCABLE — can't undo once funded
- Complex legal setup ($5,000-15,000 in fees)
- Annual administration costs
- Income payments are partially taxable

## Setup process

1. Identify charity (or donor-advised fund) as remainder beneficiary
2. Hire estate planning attorney (mandatory)
3. Choose CRAT vs CRUT
4. Fund trust BEFORE liquidity event
5. File Form 5227 annually
