# California Municipal Bonds — Double Tax-Free

## The double exclusion

California municipal bonds offer DOUBLE tax exemption:
- **Federal income tax: EXEMPT** (most U.S. munis)
- **California state income tax: EXEMPT** (if California-issued)

This makes them especially valuable for high-bracket California residents.

## Effective yield comparison

Example: $100,000 investment for California Bay Area tech worker (50% marginal rate):

| Investment | Pre-tax Yield | After-tax Yield |
|------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Treasury bond | 4.5% | 4.5% × (1 - 0.37) = 2.84% (state exempt but federal taxed) |
| Corporate bond | 5.0% | 5.0% × (1 - 0.503) = 2.49% |
| **CA Muni bond** | **3.5%** | **3.5% (both tax-free)** |

Muni bond actually beats corporate bond after taxes despite lower stated yield.

## When munis make sense

1. High California state income (top brackets, MHST)
2. High federal income (24%+ bracket)
3. Long-term holdings (matching bond duration)
4. Diversification away from market beta

## Types of California munis

- **General Obligation (GO)**: Backed by state's full taxing power
- **Revenue bonds**: Backed by specific project revenue (e.g., transit, water)
- **California Municipal Finance Authority bonds**
- **California Housing Finance Agency (CalHFA) bonds**

## Where to buy

- Mutual funds: VCAIX (Vanguard CA Intermediate-Term Tax-Exempt)
- ETFs: CMF (iShares CA Muni Bond ETF)
- Individual bonds via brokers
- New issues through underwriters

## Risks

1. **Credit risk**: Municipality could default (rare for California GO bonds)
2. **Interest rate risk**: Bond prices fall when rates rise
3. **Call risk**: Issuer may refinance early
4. **AMT exposure**: Some private activity munis trigger Alternative Minimum Tax

## Strategy for California residents

- Allocate 10-30% of bond portfolio to California munis
- Match duration to time horizon
- Use mutual funds/ETFs for diversification under $100k
- Direct ownership for larger portfolios
